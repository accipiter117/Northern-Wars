# Changelog

## M24 C A rich crown at peace
- A well-run crown at peace now makes about 1,500 crowns a month. A careless one about breaks even, and an idle one slides slowly into debt. A large army in the field for a season eats most of a year's surplus.
- The Steward's report heads Decisions and the Treasury each month: in, out, net, and the three costs that weigh most, each with a one-tap fix.
- The ledger is grouped into Taxes, Trade and sales, Crafts and goods, War and Other; tap a group to see its lines.
- Works cost their keep as they are manned, and nothing beyond the repair while they are mended.
- Conquered towns never cost the crown: they pay their garrison and works from their own takings. They yield about a quarter at first and climb to the whole as they settle and are mended; a sacked town yields almost nothing for years.
- The pressure strip warns when a kind of work is under half manned.
- Balanced now mans the crafts, mines, pastures and woods properly, the winter turn stands from the start, and hands with no winter work earn a little at home.
- Partners pay more for our goods, surplus sells better at home, gold mines yield more, the head tax is higher, and the court and the levies cost more.

## M24 B Love, Fear, Authority and Unrest
- Trust and Dread are gone. The crown is now held by Love and Fear, and Authority (the greater of the two plus a quarter of the lesser) decides how much tax is gathered, how many answer the muster, whether lords obey and whether troubled towns riot.
- A loved king gets volunteers, towns that hold out and calmer streets, but soft-seeming lords. A feared king gets taxes and levies on time, but quiet sabotage, and his grip breaks after a defeat.
- New acts: hang the ringleaders, raise gibbets at the crossroads, hold a feast and tournament, set out on a royal progress, take a hostage from a great house, and set the king's riders on the roads.
- Unrest of 30 to 50 is now ordinary grumbling. Strikes and flight from the villages only follow high Unrest when Authority is low.
- On the map close up: gibbets, heads on the gates, and bunting where the king's progress passes.
- Save version 26.

## M24 A Compact top bar
- The top bar now shows symbols and numbers instead of words: coin (with last month's change in green or red), grain, fuel, iron and cloth, then a slim row of the meters with thin bars.
- Tap any symbol for its detail; fuel, iron and cloth have new cards. Long press a symbol to see its name.
- Fits a 360px phone in one row each, and sits neatly at the top right in landscape.

## M23 D A fuller map
- The land between the towns is now full of life: patchwork fields, farmsteads, orchards and windmills where people live, thinning into copses, heath, shrines, ruins and old watchtowers where few do. Foreign realms too.
- Fields follow the year: green in spring, gold in summer, stooked at the harvest, pale in winter. They burn round a sacked town and lie bare round a hungry one.
- Close up, townsfolk walk the streets of your towns and cities, fewer in deep winter and when fear keeps them indoors.
- Nothing is drawn over roads, rivers or names; the board stays clean; and Battery saver thins it all.

## M23 C Armies and figures on the move
- Armies march as columns sized by their men, with a standard at the head, wagons at the tail and dust behind. At night they camp by the road, and in an enemy's land their foragers spread out around them.
- Your armies on the march show their route and how many days they have left. Long press one for its men, companies, marshal, spirit and arrival.
- Envoys, spies, couriers, lords and marshals now ride the roads when something real sends them: an envoy to a court, a spy into a realm, a petition to Vizima, a lord summoned to court, a marshal to his new army. Long press any of them to see who they are and when they arrive.
- From an army's card: join another army here under the marshal you choose, march to join an army elsewhere, or divide the army and send some companies off on their own.
- Save version 25.

## M23 B Labour made easy
- Labour is now the first thing in the Realm tab, and tapping the line under the top bar opens it.
- On a phone it fits one screen: a coloured bar of where the hands are (idle hands shown as a gap), five settings at a tap (Harvest, Winter, War footing, Rebuild, Balanced, or Custom once you change anything) and no more than three lines of what matters now.
- Adjust gives a slider for each kind of work, with the royal calls beneath. The old + and − buttons are gone.
- The season nudges you: harvest time in August, winter work from November, and back to the usual round afterwards.
- A town's focus is a single row of chips on its card; one tap sets it.

## M23 A Questions and Undo
- No more browser pop-ups. Weighty orders such as declaring war, breaking a treaty or taking a loan happen at once, and their notice carries an Undo button for a few seconds.
- Only what cannot be taken back asks first, in the game's own dialog: standing a company or an army down, accepting terms of peace and abandoning the reign. Cancel is the safe default; Escape or a tap outside cancels too.

## M22 D Three meters
- Authority and Stability are gone. The top bar now shows Treasury, Granary, Trust in the Crown, Unrest and Dread, all five fitting a phone held upright.
- Tap any meter to see why it moved: three to five plain reasons, biggest first.
- Trust rises with full granaries through winter, threats cleared, battles won, repairs, light taxes and relief; it falls with hunger, cold, raids left unanswered, ruined works, lost towns and battles, broken promises, heavy taxes and heavy levies. Low Trust sends people away and slows the works.
- Unrest gathers the towns' anger and the lords' mood. High Unrest brings strikes, then a great house turns against the crown.
- Dread rises with beasts, camps and Scoia'tael near our towns, plague, enemy armies and refugees, and falls with patrols, garrisons, witchers and victories. High Dread empties villages behind the walls, quietens the roads and swells the Eternal Fire; low Dread means busy roads.
- Laws, war and acts against the lords now cost Trust or stir Unrest. The crown is deposed only after months of very low Trust together with very high Unrest.
- Save version 24.

## M22 C One military
- Every soldier now belongs to a company: levies, standing companies, the lords' retinues and hired swords alike. Armies are made of companies, and each keeps its name.
- Strength is worked out one way for everything, so a garrison, a muster, a company and an army can be compared fairly.
- Levies come in companies of about 200: spearmen everywhere, crossbowmen from towns and bowmen from villages and the woods.
- A town's Military tab shows its garrison, the muster it could raise, and standing companies nearby. Call the muster to put its own people under arms in a day, to defend it or to join an army.
- Detach any company from an army, or join two armies in the same place into one.
- A city defends itself against ordinary bandits, elves and beasts, and a town against small bands. The biggest camps, war parties and famous monsters (a leshen, a griffin) now need an army or standing companies.
- Save version 23.

## M22 B Life on the map
- Everything that moves on the map now stands for something real. Merchant caravans carry what the realm sells at home; Temerian and foreign merchants carry the goods of your trade deals; pilgrims walk to a real shrine or cathedral; patrols ride for the companies you have on patrol; barges ply the rivers your towns trade on. Travellers with no reason to be there are gone.
- Long press any traveller to see what it is in one line: "Patrol: the Maribor Light cavalry on the road from Maribor to Vizima".
- Close up, the works come alive: ploughing in spring, harvesting in late summer, empty fields in winter, woodsmen felling trees, miners at the pit with a cart, shepherds with their flocks, boats on the water. A works short of hands shows fewer people; a damaged one shows a scorch mark; one being mended shows its builders.
- Hire a witcher and watch him ride out to the lair, fight, and ride home (if he wins). Long press him to see who he is and what the contract costs.

## M22 A Battery
- The map is only drawn when something needs drawing. Paused with nothing moving, the game asks for no frames at all, so it no longer drains a phone while it sits open.
- Paused with travellers in sight, the map draws a gentle 8 frames a second; running, at most 30 (full rate only while you pan or zoom).
- New Battery saver setting in Help, on by default on phones: 30 frames a second always, half the travellers on the roads, and the screen is never kept awake.
- Put away, the game draws nothing, pauses, stops the music and suspends the sound; the screen is free to sleep.

## M21 E and F Threats and clutter
- Bandits, Scoia'tael and raiders grow with the realm but stay a nuisance: no ordinary camp is larger than a town's levy or a standing company can beat. Raiders sent in war can be larger.
- Every threat says how it measures up to the nearest force: "Bandit camp: about 45 men. Gors Velen's levy: 567, overwhelming odds."
- Walls count in full until breached, for the garrison and any company stationed there. Villages still need patrols.
- The Treasury is one ledger: taxes (by towns, villages and lords), trade, sales, crafts and mines, tolls and dues, war and loans coming in; armies, upkeep, the court, imports, loans and repairs going out. The full breakdown is under Details.
- Town cards are shorter: four figures, then what the place earns, how long its food lasts and its works in one line. The full stores are under Details.
- Long lists of threats fold after eight.
- Save version 22.

## M21 C and D Hands and armies
- The Realm tab shows where the kingdom's hands are and the one most pressing shortage; tap it for the Labour screen.
- Four settings at a stroke on the Labour screen: Harvest, Winter, War footing and Rebuild, each showing what it would do before you choose ("+23 grain, −154 crowns from the crafts a month").
- Give a town a focus (Food, Iron and arms, Cloth or Timber) from its card, and its villages' hands lean that way. Spare hands now work the works harder.
- Armies of a real size: the levy is a share of each place's people (Vizima alone sends about 1,400 at a full call). The men leave the fields and workshops while they are away, and come back when you stand them down.
- Call the lords and see what each house will send first. Hire mercenary companies of 400 to 800 for a season.
- Armies are paid each month, more on campaign. An army you cannot pay grumbles, then deserts.
- The Military tab says how many men are under arms, what they cost and how long you can pay them.
- Foreign realms muster by the same rules.
- Save version 21.

## M21 A and B Trade and money
- Trade in five goods: grain, timber and stone, iron and arms, cloth, and luxuries. Treasury, Trade shows what the realm has to spare or lacks of each every month, and what it fetches. Shortages make a good dear, gluts make it cheap.
- One trade deal per partner realm: sell a good they want, buy one they offer, so much a month. Make it from a realm's card, or with the Trade order on the board. Each partner wants and offers goods from its own land, and that changes with war and bad harvests. War cuts a deal, and what it supplied grows dear.
- Whatever surplus is left sells at home each month, for less than abroad. Hold a good back with one tap (keep the iron for a war).
- Taxes by estate: the towns, the villages and the lords, each Light, Fair or Heavy, with what each would bring in shown before you choose. Heavier taxes anger whoever pays them. The old Taxation and Noble privilege laws are gone.
- War pays: loot, ransoms, coin in peace terms, tribute and requisitions each show on the ledger. A realm buys back a commander we hold after a month, if it can pay.
- Loans from the Vivaldi bank, repaid month by month with interest. Miss a payment and the rate rises and the lords take note.
- Several works of the same craft no longer earn so much less each.
- Save version 20.

## M20 D and E One map, the board of the North

- Up close, every town carries pips for its works, red where something is broken.
- The top bar follows: on the board it reads "The North", the month and how many of its three orders are set; up close it gives one line on the season ahead (first frost, grain and fuel), which opens the Labour screen.
- Tap a realm on the board for its short card, and give it an order: march an army on it, offer a trade, or send an envoy. Three orders a month, sent at its turn alongside the rulers' own moves. An envoy who arrives waits at court with an audience.

## M20 C Labour through the seasons
- The kingdom's hands go where the crown sends them, by kind of work: the fields, the woods and turf cuts, the mines, the pastures, the boats, the workshops, the masons mending what is broken, and the levies. A works yields only as well as it is manned.
- Villages a day's walk from a town lend it their hands; townsfolk go out to the villages at harvest.
- The seasons rule the work: the fields from spring to the end of harvest, the boats laid up when ice and storms close the water, the most hands to the pastures at the shearing.
- Three royal calls: the harvest muster (every hand to the fields for six weeks), the winter turn (at first frost the field hands go to the woods, the turf and the workshops) and raising the levies (fuller garrisons, emptier works).
- Fuel: wood, charcoal and turf to keep the hearths lit through winter. When the woodpiles run out, people grow angry and the poorest die of cold.
- The Labour screen, in the Realm tab, shows the year, the grain and fuel for winter, and where every hand is working.
- A works on a town's card can be kept fully manned before the rest.
- Save version 18.

## M20 A and B A kingdom already built
- The crown no longer builds. Every town, village and castle, at home and abroad, starts with the works its land gives it: a watermill on the river, boats on the lake, a quarry in the hills, peat in the marsh, the market and smithy in the town square, walls and a watchtower on the border. Vizima strikes the king's coin at its royal mint; Maribor grinds the harvest on the Ina; Flotsam and Houtborg dig silver.
- A town's card shows the land it stands on and a Works tab: each space, what stands in it, and how well.
- Works wear with use and break under raids, armies, sieges, storms, floods and fire, and faster when the crown cannot pay its bills. A works yields only as well as it stands.
- A broken works asks the crown what to do: rebuild it (whole again, but idle while the masons work), patch it up (cheaper, but it may give way in a hard winter) or leave it (free, but the place goes without and remembers).
- The old gold workings lie flooded: rebuild a gold mine to reopen its seam.
- Temeria has no sea coast. Salt and ships are Cidaris's.
- Save version 17. Old saves keep what they built, in the space where it belongs.

## M19 D The map at war
- Borders follow the towns. Take Bremervoord and the country round it turns Temerian on the map, with the old border gone and a new one drawn round what we hold.
- Battles are drawn where they are fought: banners facing, men clashing, each side's numbers over its head, and the losers fleeing. Tap one to open its card.
- Siege camps ring a besieged town with tents and works; its walls crumble as they are worn down and smoke rises once they are breached.
- Sacked towns smoulder, driven-out peoples walk the roads to their kin, and rebel banners fly over towns that have risen.
- A foreign town's card now says what it would be worth to the crown and what walls stand in the way.

## M19 C Battles and sieges you can watch
- Battles last days, not an instant: a skirmish where the archers count, the melee where men-at-arms and knights decide it and the levies break first, then the rout, where horse ride the fleeing down. Ground and weather matter: woods and marsh blunt cavalry, hills favour the defender, rain spoils the bowstrings.
- A fifth of every army waits in reserve. Commit it when the lines close for better odds and heavier losses, or sound the retreat to save what remains. Foreign captains make the same choices by their tempers.
- The Battle card shows both sides' men, spirit and losses, a bar of who is winning, and each turn of the fight in a line: a volley breaking a charge, a flank turned, levies running, a commander wounded, killed or taken for ransom.
- The game slows to 1x while our armies fight, so it can be watched (or pauses, if you prefer: see Help).
- Sieges you can follow: walls of timber or stone worn down day by day, food counted in days, sickness and desertion in the camp, sallies and relief armies. Starving garrisons offer to march out on terms and leave the town intact. Storm the walls at the odds shown, starve them out, or lift the siege.
- Armies waste away far from friendly towns, in winter, to disease in a big summer camp and to desertion when unpaid; the army card says how many and why.
- Every battle and siege leaves its report in the Chronicle.

## M19 A and B Conquest
- Towns taken in war are occupied, not owned, until a peace says otherwise. Our soldiers can strip an occupied town of grain and coin; nothing is built there. On the map an occupied town wears its occupier's colour hatched over its own.
- A town that becomes Temerian is ours for good: a full crown town with its own people (Bremervoord's stay Cidarians), their faith, buildings and stores, to be built in, taxed and garrisoned like any other. Towns we lose at a peace leave us the same way. Old reigns keep what they won.
- Take a town and decide its fate: spare it, sack it for plunder and a generation of hatred, or drive out one of its peoples, who take the road to their kin. At the peace, keep it as crown land or grant it to a great house.
- Conquered towns have an integration bar: time, a garrison, prosperity, building and tolerant laws win them over; heavy taxes, harsh laws, sacks elsewhere and an exiled ruler's whispers set them back. Settlers from Temeria speed it up, at a price. A sullen town pays less, sends fewer men and may rise.
- Risings spread to towns of the same people and can bring their old realm back to war. Crush them, or treat with them for a charter. A town that holds out for a season is free.
- Swallow a whole realm and its ruler goes into exile, a claimant whose claim fades with the years. Pay them off, send an agent against them, or ask their host to hand them over. A small realm can be made a vassal instead.
- The North fears a conqueror. Every town taken raises it, sacks and expulsions twice over; when fear runs high the realms swear a league against us, and may strike together when we are weak. Great houses whose loyalty has collapsed may defect to a neighbour, lands and all. Foreign rulers conquer, sack, annex and face risings by the same rules.
- Save version 16.

## M18 Rulers who govern
- The rulers of the North now govern. Each month they weigh the danger they face, the chances before them and the state of their purse, and settle on a course: growing, arming, preparing a war, mending their debts or scheming against a stronger neighbour. They keep to it for at least six months unless danger jumps.
- Foreign realms keep standing armies that cost them every month, and courts that sit on gold pay for it. A realm in debt sees soldiers desert and towns suffer, and its ruler may offer Temeria a loan, goods or a village for coin. Verden no longer drowns in debt, and Kerack's garrisons recover after a war.
- Foreign rulers build from the whole catalogue (markets, harbours, barracks, mines and the rest) where their land allows, choosing what pays and what suits their course. Big realms build two at once.
- Wars have a purpose: a ruler picks a town to take, gathers an army on the border for six weeks, then declares with a reason. Peace lets them keep what they came for.
- A well-defended Temeria is probed rather than invaded: sponsored raiders, tolls, demands, a show of force, slander abroad, or a pact they want paid to keep. Honourable rulers never break a pact with us; others do only when the crown looks weak.
- Pacts between the realms last longer and are not signed and broken in a single season.
- An embassy tells you a ruler's course and why; a spy tells you their war aim. Without either, you hear only rumour.
- Save version 15.
## M18 Music and sound
- A soundtrack: March of the Shadow loops behind the realm. It starts with your first tap (browsers forbid sound before one), fades in, plays on while the game is paused, fades out when the game is put away and dips under alerts and horns. On iPhones it respects the silent switch and mixes with your own audio.
- Interface sounds, all made in the browser with no sound files: a parchment tick for taps, a paper slide as the sheet opens and closes, a flick between tabs, a clock tick for the speed buttons, a wooden thunk for orders, a wax seal for laws and peace, a coin clink when the treasury moves, a dull knock when an order is refused, a hand bell for new decisions and sightings, a horn for war and sieges, and a soft tick as a long press completes. Each varies a little every time, and only a few ever sound at once.
- Help has a Sound section: music and effects sliders, and a mute for each, kept on this device.
- The music is not part of the install; the service worker keeps it after the first full download and answers Range requests, so it plays offline on Safari too.

## M17 Audiences with rulers
- Meet the rulers of the North face to face. An audience opens from the row of portraits on the Diplomacy tab, a realm's card, a ruler's request in the Court inbox, or a long press on their capital. The realm waits while you talk.
- Every ruler has a temper (greedy, honourable, ambitious, cautious, opportunist, zealous) and an agenda: Henselt wants Vergen, Calanthe wants Dillingen, Demavend wants busy roads, Ervyll wants the Scoia'tael crushed. Their mood is now a list of reasons you can read, and their trust in Temeria's word is a memory of its own.
- The deal table: lay crowns, monthly payments, goods, trade routes, sight, passage, embassies or towns on either side, and see the verdict change as you go: Acceptable, Close, They want more or Insulting. Ask what they would accept, or hear their own proposal. What is agreed is carried out: payments and deliveries each month in the ledger, caravans under treaty on the road.
- Treaties: non-aggression, trade, defensive, full alliance, military access and protectorate (Kerack, Cidaris, Verden or Brugge may place themselves under our protection and pay for it). Allies are now called to war by treaty, not by friendship alone, and breaking a treaty costs trust in every court.
- Gifts, denunciations, demands for tribute, and asking a ruler to stop massing troops, sheltering raiders or taxing our caravans. Demands succeed by fear.
- War is declared with a reason, and a war without one costs trust everywhere. Demand terms before war. Peace is made at the deal table.
- Rulers ask for audiences with real proposals, demands and warnings in place of the old random envoys, and the realms of the North make and break pacts among themselves. The Pacts screen draws the web of alliances across the North.
- Save version 14: old friendships with true allies become defensive pacts.

## M15 B A detailed North
- The North is retraced from an atlas: 24 lands with their true shapes. The eleven realms we deal with keep their rulers and ways; the thirteen further off (Kovir, Poviss, Hengfors, Lyria and Rivia, Dol Blathanna, Angren, Nazair, Mag Turga, Toussaint, Tir Tochair, Metinna, Korath and Skellige) appear as the lands beyond, named, with their towns and roads.
- 65 real towns abroad replace the invented ones (Novigrad, Oxenfurt, Gors Velen, Ban Ard, Aldersberg, Hamm, Dillingen and the rest). Temeria is unchanged.
- The country abroad is the country of the atlas: the Blue Mountains, the Kestrel Mountains, Brokilon, the Amell range, 19 rivers that wind and taper to the sea, and the lakes. Roads run round the mountains and cross the rivers near towns.
- Life abroad follows the same rules: bandit camps and monster lairs in foreign wilds that bleed the towns near them until that realm's soldiers clear them (ours do not cross the border after them); foreign villages cut timber, quarry stone or mine iron by their country, and hill and mountain towns can strike gold.
- Richer terrain everywhere: massed forests, shaded peaks, mounds, reeds and pools, lakes with shorelines, rivers named along their course. It stays visible under the fog.
- The map is painted once into tiles at several levels of detail, so the whole North pans smoothly even zoomed right out.
- Old saves carry over: towns that no longer exist hand their buildings, owners, routes, sieges and armies to the new town nearest them. Save version 13.

## M15 A Native feel
- The game behaves like an installed app: no selecting text, no copy or save menus on a long press, no dragging images, no right-click menu, no page zoom or pull-to-refresh. Buttons look pressed the instant they are touched.
- Long press is a game gesture, with a ring filling under your finger. On one of your towns, a ring of quick actions: Build, Recruit, Garrison, Trade route, Open card. On a company, its orders; on an army, Hold and Besiege; on a threat, Send nearest. On anything foreign, what is known of it; on open land, what's here. On a row in a list, a quick look without leaving the list.
- The map glides on after a flick, pinch zooms between your fingers, a double tap zooms in and a two-finger tap zooms out.
- Install it to your home screen: it has its own icon, opens full screen, starts at once and plays offline. When an update is ready, a small prompt offers to restart.
- Switching away saves the reign and pauses it; it stays paused until you play on. Help has settings for vibration (Android) and for keeping the screen awake while playing.
- Kinder to the battery: the map is never drawn denser than two pixels to one, and not at all while paused and still.

## M14 Interface overhaul
- A tab bar along the foot (down the side when the phone lies flat): Realm, Court, Treasury, Military and Diplomacy, with badges for what waits. Each tab has sub-tabs and reopens where you left it. Chronicle, Help and the saves moved to a small menu under the three dots.
- A real back button: cards opened from a list stack up, and the back arrow, the browser's back and the Android back gesture retrace the way.
- The panel never loses your place. In portrait it is a sheet with three heights, dragged by its title; a tap on the map lowers it to its title bar instead of closing it. In landscape it sits beside the map and stays open. Screens remember their sub-tab, scroll, open sections and half-filled forms, across saves too.

- Shorter cards: one layout everywhere, with key figures as chips and long text behind an (i). Town cards have Overview, Build, Military and People tabs, and Build lists only what can be raised there. Laws are one line each. Help is a searchable list. Small icons beside the meters.
- Fewer taps: tap a meter to see why it moved. Targets are picked on the map (hunts, patrols, stations, trade routes, sieges, the muster place) with the odds or the value shown before you confirm. Send nearest suitable company on every threat; Recruit here and Trade route from here on every town.

## M13 A realm under arms
- Threats are things on the map. Bandit camps grow in the forests, marshes and hills near poor and restless places and send bands to hold the roads (caravans stop, crown routes and market trade are robbed) and fall on weak villages. Monster lairs hold drowners, wolves, harpies and wyverns, or something far worse. Scoia'tael commandos come out of the woods when the elves are angry; hostile neighbours send war parties over the border.
- What the crown's eyes reach it sees clearly; beyond them, rumours. A short alert names each sighting, and Help can pause the game on sightings.
- Standing companies: militia, archers, men-at-arms, crossbowmen, light cavalry and knights, recruited from a town's card. They draw on the town's people, the crown's stores of arms and horses, and the treasury every month; they have morale, wounds and experience, and desert when unpaid.
- Standing orders: hold a place, patrol a road, guard a trade route, hunt a sighted threat or enemy army, or stand ready to answer anything sighted nearby. Odds in plain words before a hunt; fights settle themselves, with a marker on the map and a report in the Chronicle. Witchers can be hired for the worst beasts.
- Field armies can take standing companies along, and fight better for them.
- Banner tokens for every force, sized by strength, with men and morale close up; camps and lairs drawn in the terrain; lines to where forces are going; a Forces screen with every company and threat and a button to send the nearest company.
- Old patrols become companies of light horse on the same roads. Save version 12.

## M12 A working economy
- A rescaled economy: a well-run crown now prospers instead of living in debt. Taxes depend on the kind of place and grow with prosperity and with market halls, guildhalls and merchants' quarters. Crafts earn several times more, by the size and fortunes of their place (a little less each the more of one trade the realm has). Gold mines yield about 150 a month at their peak. Patrols cost by the length of their road. Debt interest is 1% a month, but deep debt now stirs unrest.
- Production chains: sheep pastures and weavers' halls (wool to cloth), rye distilleries (spare grain to rye spirit), charcoal burners and weaponsmiths (timber and iron to arms, dearest while the realms around are at war), salt pans on the Velen coast (salt, and stores that keep longer), and horse studs on the southern farmland (horses, and cheaper patrols). Finished goods ride the crown's routes at a premium where the far end lacks them, most of all abroad, and the rest sells at home. Carts carry them along the roads.
- New trades: silver mines in the hills, toll bridges at the White Bridge and Devil's Ford that earn from every caravan crossing the Pontar, a shipyard at Gors Velen, a fishery and smokehouse, merchants' quarters in the cities, a pilgrims' hostel at the Temple of Melitele, an alchemist's workshop (the Eternal Fire disapproves) and a hunters' lodge that thins the beasts nearby. Each stands only where the land suits it, with sheep, horses, boats and smoke about it.
- The Treasury (formerly the Ledger): a line for every crown in and out, and the places that earn and cost most. Every town's card says what it earned and cost the crown last month. The first time the treasury goes into the red, a hint explains debt and opens the Treasury.
- The Trade screen shows the goods each route carries and what the chains made; patrols show their cost.
- tools/longrun.mjs can print each year's ledger; the model crowns live in tools/crowns.mjs; tools/payback.mjs covers every new building.
- Save version 11.

## M11 Trade, gold and a busier realm
